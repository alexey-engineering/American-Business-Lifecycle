# Project brief — American Business Lifecycle

## Stakeholder

Market Intelligence / Strategy Director.

## Main question

Where is the U.S. business ecosystem growing, contracting, and changing—and which industries and geographies are driving those changes?

## Questions

1. Which states have experienced the strongest employment growth?
2. Which states have experienced the strongest employment contraction?
3. Which industries are driving employment growth?
4. Which industries are contracting?
5. How do young and mature firms differ in employment dynamics?
6. How do firm-size categories differ in employment dynamics?
7. How did business dynamics change before, during, and after the COVID-19 period?

## Minimum useful scope

- **Geography:** United States and 50 states plus District of Columbia; no counties, metros, or territories.
- **Industry:** 2-digit NAICS sector groups; no detailed industries.
- **Firm age:** official BDS categories, grouped for comparison into young (0–5 years), mature (6+ years), and left-censored.
- **Firm size:** ten official firm employment bands (1–4 through 10,000+ employees).
- **Time:** annual 1978–2023 source series. BDS year *t* compares March 12 employment in *t−1* and *t*. COVID comparison uses 2015–2020 pre-shock, 2021 shock, and 2022–2023 rebound. The recession state window is 2008–2010 because 2007 ends before the recession contraction.
- **Crossings:** one-way BDS tables only. State, industry, firm age, and firm size comparisons are independent margins; the project does not claim state-by-industry or age-by-size detail.

## Measures

Employment, job creation/destruction, net job creation and rate, establishment births/deaths, and supporting firm/establishment counts. BDS's annual net job creation rate is used as the employment growth rate proxy; no causal interpretation is made.
